    PharmAtlas Help Center

    A practical guide for setting up members, scheduling medications, documenting administrations, managing inventory, preparing labels, tracking monitoring values, and keeping backup records.

    1. Recommended Setup
    1. Add each person in the Members tab.
    2. Add providers, pharmacies, diagnoses, allergies, intolerance notes, monitoring goals, height/weight/BMI details, and contact information in each member profile.
    3. Add inventory items before scheduling them. Use formulation, stock unit, give/apply unit, expiration date, storage, barcode, and assignment fields.
    4. Create regimens for grouped routines such as supplements, rescue plans, or condition-specific medication groups.
    5. Make a backup before heavy editing or before testing a major workflow.
    2. Scheduling Medications
    • Use Schedule Medications to stage medications before adding them to a profile.
    • Pending scheduled medications can be reviewed, modified, removed, and then finalized.
    • Supported frequencies include one-time, daily, BID, TID, QID, nightly, PRN, every other day, weekly, every X weeks, X times per week, monthly, and every X hours.
    • Every X hours schedules preview upcoming times and can adjust future times after late/early documentation.
    • Use linked medications when items must be taken together or when only one formulation should be selected.
    • Regimen therapy days let one medication run for a smaller window inside a larger regimen, such as days 1-5 of a treatment cycle.
    • Medication, current-list, and regimen PDF/Print tools open a built-in preview where medications and columns can be included or excluded.
    3. Documenting Doses
    • Use Administer from a member profile, regimen, calendar, active order, PRN order, or inventory Take button.
    • Document the actual date/time, and mark doses as given, late, early, skipped, or missed.
    • Purple timeline blocks mean a dose was given at a different time than originally scheduled.
    • Skipped and missed doses remain in the administration record and affect adherence.
    • If a medication exists in a Med Kit, the administration dialog lets you choose whether stock came from main inventory or from that kit.
    4. Progress Notes / Diary
    • Use each member profile's Progress Notes / Diary tab for daily observations, care handoffs, symptoms, side effects, provider calls, and lab follow-up.
    • Templates can start common note types, and custom diary templates can be created or edited for your own workflows.
    • Rich text tools support bold, italics, underline, lists, and text color.
    • Notes can be pinned, marked important, flagged for follow-up, marked private for caregiver-only context, or marked resolved.
    • Smart tag suggestions help organize notes. Search and tag/flag filters narrow the list quickly.
    • Notes can link back to medications, administrations, monitoring readings, lab PDFs, regimens, or inventory items.
    • Pain, mood, sleep, nausea, and energy scales can be saved with each entry for trend context.
    • Images and PDFs can be attached. Diary reports can be printed or saved as PDFs by date range, with private notes excluded unless selected.
    5. Inventory Basics
    • Inventory tracks name, strength, formulation, quantity, expiration, storage, control level, notes, photos, barcode aliases, prescriber, and member assignment.
    • For tablets, capsules, mL, sprays, drops, and similar items, administered doses reduce stock.
    • For container-based products such as many topicals, stock can stay unchanged until the container is marked finished.
    • Use Restock to add received quantities directly to inventory.
    • Use Print Inventory Labels or individual Label buttons to generate inventory labels.
    6. Recipes & Compounding
    • Use Recipes to create reusable compounding formulas with ingredients, yield, storage, beyond-use dating, instructions, and prep notes.
    • The prep panel shows ingredient requirements, barcode/QR scan checks, bottle label preview, lot details, and completed batch history.
    • Completing a prep subtracts marked ingredients from inventory and adds the finished compounded preparation to Inventory.
    7. Med Kits
    • Use Inventory > Med Kits to create named kits such as Travel Kit, Emergency Kit, School Kit, Work Kit, or First Aid Kit.
    • Search inventory items, enter a quantity, and add them into the selected kit.
    • Adding an item to a kit subtracts that quantity from main inventory.
    • Use Restock to add more of an existing kit item, Adjust Quantity to correct counts, and Return/Discard to choose whether removed stock goes back to main inventory.
    • Kits and kit items turn red when any item is expired or expiring within 10 days.
    8. Formulary & Orders
    • The formulary is a master reorder list. It can include inventory items and non-inventory items.
    • Create Orders can add low/expired items, formulary items, current inventory, or new non-inventory items.
    • New non-inventory order items are highlighted yellow until received and entered into inventory.
    • The Order Log lets you edit active orders, delete order records, generate PDFs, and modify received order records.
    • Prices are line prices for the ordered item, not price multiplied by tablets, mL, capsules, or units.
    9. Prepackaging & Labels
    • Prepack queues can be created for date ranges and later printed when it is time to prepare doses.
    • Use scanning when available, or use manual completion when a scanner is not available.
    • Prepack labels can be grouped by all times, daytime, or evening.
    • If medications change after labels are generated, affected prepacks are marked for reprint.
    • Inventory labels can include QR/barcode codes for verification workflows.
    10. Monitoring & Reports
    • Monitoring supports BP/HR, blood glucose, weight, temperature, INR, drug levels, and custom lab goals.
    • Member profiles display monitoring graphs, adherence, calendars, active orders, PRN orders, completed schedules, and administration logs.
    • Medication/Supplement summaries show active scheduled and PRN items, inventory forecasts, and last documented doses.
    • PDFs are available for inventory, schedules, monitoring, progress notes/diary, formulary, and inventory orders.
    • Schedule PDFs can be used as paper check-off sheets for trips, caregivers, or periods away from the app.
    11. Today, Audit & Health
    • Today View shows overdue, due-soon, later, completed, and PRN items for quick daily work.
    • Setup Guide walks through a first-use checklist and can jump directly to the needed tab.
    • Hover help adds small question-mark tips near common controls and can be disabled from the Dashboard.
    • Data Health Check looks for broken schedule links, orphaned records, duplicate barcodes, missing times, and impossible stock counts.
    • Audit Trail records safety-relevant changes such as scheduling, discontinuing, administration, restocking, profile edits, and overrides. Medication rows also have an Audit button for order-specific history.
    • Printer Calibration previews a test label so label borders, font size, and scanner text can be checked before a large print run.
    12. Local Sync & Privacy
    • Desktop Sync Folder can place PharmAtlas data inside OneDrive, Google Drive Desktop, Dropbox, or another user-controlled synced folder.
    • Avoid editing from two computers at the same time. Let the sync app finish before opening PharmAtlas elsewhere.
    • PharmAtlas data, PDFs, labels, backups, synced folders, and emails may contain private health information.
    • Protect your computer account, cloud account, email app password, backups, and exported files.
    Safety Checklist Before Real Use
    Troubleshooting
    Text or windows look cramped:Use the app at normal desktop scaling when possible. Wide windows and taskbar-minimized workspaces are designed for desktop use.
    Printer preview is unavailable:Use PharmAtlas label preview first, then print. Some embedded print engines do not expose a native preview screen.
    Inventory stock looks wrong:Check whether the dose was documented from main inventory, a Med Kit, or a container-finish workflow.
    Order item is yellow:It is a new non-inventory item and needs full product details during receiving.
    Administration history changed after editing:Use the timeline and administration log to edit documentation rather than recreating schedules whenever possible.
    Important Note

    PharmAtlas is an organization and documentation tool. It does not replace a licensed prescriber, pharmacist, clinical medication review, or emergency care. Always verify medication instructions, vaccine decisions, and monitoring goals with qualified healthcare professionals.
